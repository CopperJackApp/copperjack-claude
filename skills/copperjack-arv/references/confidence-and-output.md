# Confidence and the six answers

## Confidence means strength of evidence

Rate how strongly the available facts and comparable sales support the stated ARV and range. A 4/5 is an evidence rating, not an 80% probability of achieving a particular sale price. A wide range alone does not earn high confidence.

| Rating | Evidence pattern |
|---|---|
| **5 — Very High** | Clear property facts, multiple near-twin renovated closed sales, little adjustment uncertainty, and no material unresolved issue. |
| **4 — High** | Clear facts and at least two strong renovated closed sales, or a near-twin strongly corroborated by other closed evidence. Normal, understandable differences do not materially undermine the conclusion. |
| **3 — Medium** | Useful evidence with meaningful adjustments or a material unresolved property/comp difference. The estimate is useful but sensitive to specific assumptions. |
| **2 — Low** | Thin comparable evidence or major unresolved facts. Several plausible interpretations could move the estimate materially. |
| **1 — Very Low** | Core facts or credible market evidence are missing, unreliable, or unsuitable for a defensible estimate. |

Use the full scale. High confidence does not require an appraisal or a perfect duplicate; normal qualitative adjustments do not automatically imply low confidence. Conversely, repeated listings of the same sale and several calculations from the same comps do not constitute independent corroboration.

Report overall confidence plus compact **property-fact** and **comp-evidence** ratings. Explain the actual weak link rather than mechanically averaging them. A material unresolved living-area split or uncertain comp condition must remain visible even if other inputs look strong.

## Result structure

Lead with the address, analysis date, **ARV**, **range**, and **overall confidence**. Use CopperJack's response format when the host supplies one; otherwise use concise Markdown. Keep the following six answers identifiable. There is no required HTML file, downloadable report, or separate JSON payload.

1. **ARV.** State the best-supported estimate and a brief thesis identifying the main evidence. Include the few decisive calculation lines or qualitative adjustments that explain the conclusion. State the retail-updates assumption once. When unsupported, explicitly state that the estimate cannot yet be established.

2. **ARV range.** State low and high and briefly explain what supports each end. Keep the point estimate within the range. Distinguish a working estimate from a more reliable conclusion. Do not invent bounds when no credible price evidence exists.

3. **Confidence.** Show the overall score/label, property-fact score, comp-evidence score, and a short explanation connected to this property's actual evidence. Avoid generic caveats or treating ordinary resale variation as a defect in every analysis.

4. **Improve confidence.** List up to three high-value remaining actions, each paired with the uncertainty it resolves. For example, "Obtain the subject's lower-level photos to compare daylight and usable ceiling height with the primary comps." Complete useful available tool research first. If the evidence is already strong and no material gaps remain, say so; do not manufacture tasks. Keep this answer present at every confidence level.

5. **Primary comps and why.** For each, show address or supplied identifier, closed price/date, distance if known, above-grade sf, below-grade sf, relevant structural/finish facts, inclusion reason, differences from the subject, and relative influence. Include total finished sf when helpful and clearly label price-per-area denominators if used. A compact table plus short explanations is sufficient. Use fewer comps when only fewer qualify.

6. **Excluded comps and why.** Account for rejected candidates actually considered, including conspicuous nearby/recent sales and duplicate records. Give each an identifier, a correctly status-labeled price if known, and its specific exclusion reason. Keep secondary closed benchmarks and non-closed directional listings separately labeled alongside this accounting; explain why they do not control the value. When there are no exclusions, say "No candidates excluded"; when none could be evaluated, say that instead. Never invent a rejected candidate to fill the answer.

Above/below-grade pricing belongs in the comp comparisons and valuation explanation: identify a meaningful difference and its treatment, or briefly state that the matched splits require no separate adjustment. Do not force separate dollar contributions when the market evidence cannot isolate them.

Keep the explanation easy to scan without removing necessary reasoning. Plain sentences, short bullets and small tables are preferable to arbitrary fragment limits or a large repeated report. Identify supporting comp/record IDs, photo numbers or actual links where useful; never invent citations. Describe missing evidence accurately and report the actual search limitations when they affect the result.
